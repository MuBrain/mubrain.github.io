# MuBrain #2 — Who Owns an Organization's Intelligence?

## Intent

Explore the boundary between what an organization can legitimately retain and what remains attached to the individual, especially when people and agents contribute together to collective work.

## Working questions

- What belongs to the collective, and what remains individual?
- How can an organization onboard individual intelligence without claiming to own it?
- How can a person be offboarded without losing the critical context they helped create?
- How do AI agents change these boundaries?

## Format

MuBrain 5@7, from 5:00 p.m. to 7:00 p.m., 100% in person in Montreal. Date and venue to be confirmed.

## LinkedIn description

Who owns an organization's intelligence?

Some of it lives in systems, processes, and documents.

Some of it lives in people: their experience, relationships, intuition, and implicit understanding of context.

And now, some of that intelligence may also be carried by agents.

This second MuBrain meetup will explore the boundaries between individual intelligence and collective intelligence.

How can a person genuinely enrich the collective without the organization pretending to absorb what belongs to that person?

How can what should remain individual be preserved while decisions, reasoning, and learning that matter to the organization are retained?

And conversely, how can we prevent a departure from taking with it a critical part of the organization's memory?

We will discuss the onboarding and offboarding of intelligence, governance, contribution, memory, and the emerging role of AI agents.

As always at MuBrain, the objective is not to deliver a top-down conference. We want to confront perspectives and progressively build a shared research framework.
